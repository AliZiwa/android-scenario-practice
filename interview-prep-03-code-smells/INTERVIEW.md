# Assignment 03: the questions this diff invites

Answer each out loud in under two minutes. These are the follow-ups a Staff interviewer would ask after reading your PR.

1. In round 2, `ShiftRow` computed `Utils.calcPay(shift, rate = 42.0)` while the header used `rateProvider.getRate()`. Describe what a technician at `hourly_rate = 55` saw, which test should have caught it, and why `ShiftUiModel.pay` is the fix rather than passing the rate into the row.
2. You introduced `RateProvider` as a `fun interface` and kept `Utils.rate(context: Context? = null)` with a catch-all returning 42.0. What calls the second one now? What is the cost of leaving it, and what would a `Deprecated` annotation buy you here versus deleting it?
3. You said the brief was "no behaviour changes". Define behaviour for this app in one sentence. Then tell me why `submitAllDrafts` going concurrent in round 1 broke it, and why putting it back is not just a revert but the right design: what changes for a backend that rate-limits per client, and for the order of server ids?
4. In round 1 the committed `1.json` had no `tmp` column and the same `identityHash` as `2.json`. Explain how Room's schema export produced that, what a `MigrationTestHelper` test built from that file would have proved, and what you did about it.
5. `catch (e: Exception)` in `ShiftRepository.submit` wraps a suspend call. The ViewModel is cleared while a submit is in flight. What does the catch do with the `CancellationException`, and what is returned to a caller that no longer exists? Why is this the same comment as assignment 02?
6. The ViewModel's constructor defaults to `Injector.repository` and `Injector.rateProvider`. Is the service locator gone? What does `viewModel()` in Compose actually call, why does that compile without a factory, and what breaks the first time a second ViewModel needs a dependency `Injector` does not have?
7. `ShiftRow` now takes `ShiftUiModel` and two lambdas. `ShiftUiModel` holds a `ShiftEntity` with `var` properties. What does Compose's stability inference make of that model, what does it do to recomposition of a 200-row list, and what is the one-line change that makes the model stable?
8. `submit(shift)` mutates `serverId` and `status` on the entity object that the UI list is currently displaying, then writes it to Room, then the ViewModel re-fetches. Which of those three is the source of truth at each step, and where could a reader observe an intermediate state?
9. `ShiftDao.observeAll()` returns a `Flow` and nothing calls it; the ViewModel re-fetches after every write. What would the refactor to `stateIn` over that flow look like, which of your tests would still pass unchanged, and which rows in your catalog does it retire?
10. Your "Already submitted" test asserts `api.submitted.size == 0`. Why is that assertion stronger than checking the message, and what kind of regression does it catch that the message check would not?
11. Row 12 said `tmp` was deleted while "What you deliberately left" said it was retained, and it stayed that way through two review rounds. A reviewer finds that. What do they do with the other eleven rows, and what is the one-line process rule that prevents it?
12. Pick the fix in your catalog you would least like to defend in a postmortem and describe the incident. Your risk column now says `Medium` for three of them; which of those three is actually the riskiest and why?
13. Round 1 was one commit of 774 files; round 2 was one commit named "commit message for fixes phase 2"; round 3 was "Fix: Pass pay per shift and lambdas into ShiftRow". The brief said one smell per commit with tests green after each. Reconstruct, on a whiteboard, the eight commits this branch should have had, in order, with their messages.
14. `assertEquals(605.0, state.totalPay, 0.001)` replaced `assertTrue(state.totalPay > 0.0)`. Show the arithmetic, then name the class of bug the inequality could never catch and the one the equality still cannot.
15. The `.gitignore` on `main` had `/build` and the branch now has `build/` and `app/build/`. What is the difference between the two patterns, why did the first one let 759 files through, and what does `git rm -r --cached` do that `git rm` does not?
